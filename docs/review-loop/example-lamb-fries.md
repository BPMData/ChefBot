Recipe Title: Greek-Style Loaded Lamb Fries with Tzatziki

Serves: 3 to 4 (as a generous main)

Total time: about 45 minutes

**Ingredients**

For the fries:
- 1 kg (2.2 lb) frozen French fries (or about 900 g / 2 lb fresh russet potatoes, cut into 1 cm / 1/2 inch sticks)
- 15 g (1 Tbsp) olive oil, if using fresh potatoes
- 2 g (about 1/2 tsp) fine salt

For the spiced lamb:
- 600 g (1 lb 5 oz) ground lamb (about 150-200 g raw per person)
- 1 medium onion (about 150 g), finely diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 Tbsp (15 ml) neutral oil, only if the lamb is very lean
- 2 tsp (4 g) ground cumin
- 1 tsp (2 g) sweet paprika
- 1/2 tsp (1 g) ground cinnamon
- 1/2 tsp (1 g) dried oregano
- 6 g (about 1 tsp) kosher salt, plus more to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, about 20 grinds (roughly 1/2 tsp)
- 1 x 400 g (15 oz) can chickpeas, drained, rinsed, and patted very dry (about 240 g drained)
- 60 ml (1/4 cup) water

For the toppings:
- 200 g (2 cups) shredded cheese: a mix of Monterey Jack and cheddar melts best; feta also works crumbled on after baking
- 2 medium tomatoes (about 300 g total), diced
- 2 jalapeños, thinly sliced (seed them for less heat)
- 250 g (about 1 cup) tzatziki sauce

**Instructions**

1. Heat the oven. Arrange a rack in the upper third of the oven and heat to 230 C / 450 F. A hot oven with convection (or fan) on, if you have it, is what keeps frozen fries crisp instead of soggy.

2. Cook the fries. Spread the frozen fries in a single layer on a large sheet pan (about 33 x 45 cm / 13 x 18 inch), preferably on a wire rack set inside the pan for maximum air circulation. Bake 25-30 minutes, flipping halfway, until deeply golden. If using fresh potatoes, toss them with the olive oil and salt first and add 5-10 minutes to the bake time. Leave the oven on.

3. Start the lamb while the fries bake. Heat a large skillet (12 inch / 30 cm) over medium-high heat. Add the oil if your lamb is lean; otherwise the lamb renders plenty of fat. Cook the onion 3-4 minutes, stirring, until softened.

4. Brown the lamb. Push the onion to the edges, add the lamb to the center, and cook undisturbed 3 minutes so it browns, then break it up and add the garlic, cumin, paprika, cinnamon, oregano, salt, and pepper. Cook 4-5 minutes more, stirring, until browned with no pink remaining. Ground lamb is fully cooked at 71 C / 160 F if you check with a thermometer.

5. Crisp the chickpeas. Stir the dried chickpeas into the lamb with the 60 ml water, scraping up the browned bits. Simmer 4-5 minutes until the chickpeas are hot and the pan is nearly dry. Turn the heat to low to hold.

6. Load the fries. Transfer the hot fries to a 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13 inch baking dish or leave them on the sheet pan. Scatter the lamb and chickpeas over the fries, then the shredded cheese, then half the jalapeño slices. Bake 5-7 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling.

7. Finish and serve. Top with the diced tomatoes, the remaining raw jalapeño slices (raw slices on top give fresher heat against the baked ones), and generous drizzles of tzatziki. Serve immediately, straight from the dish.

**Notes**
- Crispness rule: everything wet (tomatoes, tzatziki) goes on after baking. Cheese and meat go under the broiler heat so the fries stay crunchy underneath.
- Scaling up: for 6 people, use 900 g lamb and 1.5 kg fries and split everything between two sheet pans; the baking time stays the same because the pan surface area, not the quantity, drives it.
- The yellow rice from your list makes a great base underneath if you want to stretch this into rice-and-fries bowls, but it's optional here.
